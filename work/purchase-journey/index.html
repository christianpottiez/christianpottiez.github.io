Reframing the Digital Purchase Journey

What started as a question about the model page on Porsche.com evolved into a cross-market foundational study of the entire vehicle purchase journey.

01
The challenge

One page is not the journey.

The product team initially wanted to understand the experience on the model page only. I proposed expanding the scope to the full purchase journey to avoid optimising one touchpoint without understanding the bigger picture. Together we built organisational support and secured the budget for a cross-market study.

02
Our approach

In-the-moment, journey-adaptive interviews across three markets.

We designed the study around where participants actually were in their purchase journey, capturing decisions as they unfolded and adapting each interview to the individual context.

Frame the journeyDefine the stages and questions across the end-to-end purchase process.
Recruit in-marketRecruit participants while actively considering or purchasing a vehicle.
Interview from current stageExplore what they had experienced, were deciding and expected to do next.
Cross-market analysisCompare patterns across Germany, the US and China.
Synthesize into journey modelTurn recurring needs and decisions into a shared design foundation.
Why this mattered: Studying participants while they were still in the purchase process captured real decision-making context that is difficult to reconstruct retrospectively.
03
What we learned and how it changed the journey

1. Configurator used differently than assumed

It was used at a different point in the journey than previously assumed.

Role and placement of the configurator were revised in the journey design.

2. A recurring decision logic emerged

A clear pattern in how customers make decisions throughout the purchase process.

The journey was structured around the actual sequence of customer decisions.

3. Information needs change by journey phase

Different stages require different types of information and levels of detail.

Content on the model page and across touchpoints was reprioritized accordingly.

4. Different purchase patterns were identified

Six distinct buyer types with different behaviours, needs and focus areas.

The study created a shared foundation for teams designing the end-to-end experience.

05
Impact

The study became the foundation for the digital purchase journey.

The research established a shared model of how customers move through the purchase process, which information they need at different stages and how digital touchpoints should support those decisions. It became a foundational reference for journey design, content prioritisation and touchpoint planning across teams.

What started as a question about one model page became a cross-functional research foundation used beyond the team that originally commissioned the study.
